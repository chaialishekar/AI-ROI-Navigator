From the decision to go-live

Decide said "pilot it, after fixes". This is that plan.

Step 1 · Close the gaps
    Step 2 · Pilot on one line

    Doc warns on one production line while the rest of the plant runs as today. It has to pass all three exit checks:

    • Catches at least 80% of breakdowns
    • No more than one false alarm a week
    • At least half its alerts are real
    Step 3 · Go live plant-wide

    Only once every check passes. Then the monthly tracking on Measure takes over, against the a year the business case planned.

    The exit checks are the same lines the board uses on the Business case page. The pilot and the year after it are simulated for this case study.

    What got built

    A working model, a real data path, and two GenAI experiments.

    The model

    Gradient boosting

    Trained on records, tested on it never saw. Compared with a simpler, explainable baseline.

    The data path

    S3 → Databricks

    Monthly results land in Amazon S3 and flow through bronze, silver and gold tables in Databricks. Bad files are quarantined, and a re-run loads nothing twice.

    Side experiments

    GenAI on Amazon Bedrock

    Drafting work orders from warnings (30 cases) and reviewing a business case for mistakes (19 cases), each graded against known answers.

    The details

    The GenAI tests, scored.

    Drafting repair orders from warnings · 30 test cases

    Version 1 named "overstrain" 23 times, 17 of them with readings below the limit: fluent, but it can't compare numbers. Version 2 moved the diagnosis into code and left the AI to write. A person still reviews every draft.

    Reviewing a business case · 19 test cases

    Every gain came from the same split: code does the maths and checks, the AI explains. I wrote the test cases knowing the mistakes, so it is not a blind test.
