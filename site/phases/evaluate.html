What I did

Priced every AI in the library against this plant's losses.

The provider's library has nine AI tools. Three fit this plant. For each one I took the improvement it achieved elsewhere, from a published source, and applied it to the plant's own loss. Then I subtracted what the tool costs to set up and run, over three years.

  • Expected view: the middle of each published range.
  • Cautious view: the low end. An option that only wins on the expected view is a risk, not a pick.
  • Every source is labelled by how strong its evidence is, from independent study to vendor claim.

The three AI options

Same plant, same rules, three answers.

Are numbers from other companies comparable?

Not directly, and I didn't treat them as if they were. The improvement figures come from different companies, industries and kinds of study (a McKinsey survey, a vendor write-up of BMW's program). A plant that cut downtime 40% had its own machines, data and crew.

  • Only the percentage travels. Each figure is applied to this plant's own losses, not copied in dollars from someone else.
  • Ranges, not single numbers. The cautious view uses the low end, and a pick has to survive it.
  • Evidence labels. A vendor's claim counts for less than an independent study, so Lens is marked weaker.
  • Checked against our own data. Doc is also tested on machine records, and the pilot measures the real figure before full rollout. On Measure, the plant reached about 70% of the model's estimate, which is the kind of gap this caution is for.

The tools are compared only with each other for one client. I never compare one industry's results with another's.

The details

Every option, every number.

How each number is worked out. Value a year = the cost of the problem the tool targets × the improvement published elsewhere (the middle of the range; the low end for the cautious view), minus the running cost. 3-year net = value a year × the months it runs in 3 years, minus setup. Setup and time to go live grow when the client's data is weaker than the tool needs.
